Syllabus

PHYS 5211 · Introduction to Scientific Computing in the Era of AI · Fall 2026

Course description

This course introduces the numerical methods that underlie scientific computing — floating-point arithmetic, linear algebra, interpolation, differentiation and integration, optimization, and differential equations — and extends that foundation into the methods that power modern machine learning: automatic differentiation, gradient-based optimization, and numerical stability in large models. AI tools are integrated throughout as required components of coursework, not as a replacement for understanding the mathematics.

Meeting times & location

Tuesdays and Thursdays, 2:00–3:30 p.m., 177 Huntington Ave, Room 207. (The first class of the semester met on a Friday as a one-time exception.)

Textbook

Michael T. Heath, Scientific Computing: An Introductory Survey, 2nd edition. Additional readings assigned as needed for topics extending beyond the text (automatic differentiation, mixed-precision arithmetic, ML reproducibility).

Assessment philosophy

Every assignment requires the use of an AI tool (Claude, ChatGPT, or Gemini) as part of the solution process. Grades are not based on whether the final output is correct — they are based on your explanation of what you did, why the method works, and where it can fail. Expect to explain your homework verbally or in writing to the instructor or to peers.

Topics covered

  • Floating-point representation, rounding error, and numerical stability
  • Systems of linear equations and matrix conditioning
  • Interpolation, approximation, and numerical differentiation/integration
  • Automatic differentiation (reverse-mode) as a numerical methods topic, including gradient checking against finite differences
  • Vanishing and exploding gradients; numerically stable softmax
  • Mixed-precision arithmetic and quantization error
  • Optimization methods and their convergence behavior
  • Ordinary differential equations and numerical solvers
  • Reproducibility failures in machine learning pipelines
